Return to Play After UCL Reconstruction

RETURN TO PLAY · UCL & THE ELBOW · 157 FOUND · PULLED 2026-11-02

The ulnar collateral ligament (UCL) is the ligament on the inside of the elbow — the one repaired in Tommy John surgery. How often players return, and how long it takes, sets the real cost of a pitcher's roster spot.

PubMed query: "ulnar collateral ligament" AND reconstruction AND "return to play"

Tommy John surgery outcomes: what 21 studies and 2,452 patients show

Finding. Across 21 studies, return-to-play rates after UCL reconstruction ranged from 62.5% to 100%, with returns at 9.5 to 18.5 months. Outcome and satisfaction scores were favorable; complication rates varied widely, from 0% to 31.8%.

Jackson GR, Mowers CC, Sachdev D, Knapik DM, Lapica H, Sabesan VJ. Ulnar Collateral Ligament Reconstruction Is Commonly Performed Using a Palmaris Graft and Provides Favorable Patient Outcomes With Variable Return-to-Play and Postoperative Complication Rates: A Systematic Review. Arthroscopy. 2025;41(4):1099-1112.e1. doi:10.1016/j.arthro.2024.03.039 PubMed

Donor grafts for UCL reconstruction: outcomes review

Finding. In five studies of donor-tissue grafts, 95.3% of patients returned to play and no graft ruptures were reported. The authors noted a lack of high-quality evidence comparing donor grafts with a patient's own tissue in elite throwers.

Peebles LA, Blackwood NO, Verma A, O'Brien MJ, Lintner DM, Kraeutler MJ. Medial Ulnar Collateral Ligament Reconstruction With Allograft Provides Excellent Clinical Outcomes, High Rates of Return to Play, and a Low Incidence of Postoperative Complications: A Systematic Review. Arthroscopy. 2025;41(4):1113-1122. doi:10.1016/j.arthro.2024.03.045 PubMed

Rehab timing after UCL injury: earlier milestones, earlier return

Finding. Across 105 articles, rehab protocols varied widely. After reconstruction, patients who reached the early milestones sooner — motion, strengthening, throwing — returned to play earlier, with no negative effect on outcome scores.

Buchanan TR, Hones KM, Hao KA, et al. Rehabilitation Protocols in Elbow Medial Ulnar Collateral Ligament Injuries: A Systematic Review of Articles Published in the Last 20 Years. Sports Health. 2025;17(3):460-469. doi:10.1177/19417381241249125 PubMed

Injury Prevention and Workload

INJURY PREVENTION & WORKLOAD · BASEBALL · 426 FOUND · PULLED 2026-11-02

Most pitching injuries build over time rather than arriving on one pitch. This topic tracks the workload and body-mechanics research a club can act on before an arm breaks down.

PubMed query: baseball AND ("injury prevention" OR workload)

Hip motion and injury risk in baseball players

Finding. Healthy youth and high school players had slightly more hip rotation than injured players. The authors concluded the differences were small and the studies mixed, so no strong recommendation can be made yet.

Paul RW, Anton B, Meloche M, et al. Effect of Hip Range of Motion Restrictions on Injury in Baseball Players: A Systematic Review. J Athl Train. 2026;61(8):543-551. doi:10.4085/1062-6050-0715.25 PubMed

How a pitcher's throwing shoulder changes over time

Finding. Measured in a way that isolates soft tissue, the throwing shoulder's limits showed up in outward rotation rather than inward. The authors concluded that reported inward-rotation losses are not as common as once thought.

Paul RW, Sirch FR, Vata A, et al. Chronic Adaptations of the Shoulder in Baseball Pitchers: A Systematic Review. Am J Sports Med. 2025;53(12):2984-2994. doi:10.1177/03635465251317202 PubMed

Pitch-count jumps and velocity drops linked to arm injuries in college pitchers

Finding. In 187 collegiate pitchers followed for one summer, arm injury risk rose when a pitcher's pitch counts climbed above his own usual workload. Injuries were also linked to drops in fastball velocity.

Salamh P, Plisky P, Beiting J, et al. Association of Changes in Game Pitch Count, Pitching Velocity, and Arm Injuries in Collegiate Pitchers. Am J Sports Med. 2026:3635465261489882. doi:10.1177/03635465261489882 PubMed

Muscle Regeneration and Cellular Recovery

CELLULAR RECOVERY · MUSCLE REGENERATION · 404 FOUND · PULLED 2026-11-02

Muscle repair runs on satellite cells — the muscle's resident stem cells — and the immune signals that switch them on. This is the recovery science underneath every rehab timeline the club plans around.

PubMed query: ("skeletal muscle regeneration" OR "satellite cells") AND (injury OR exercise) AND recovery

How exercise retrains the immune system to help muscle regrow

Finding. The authors described exercise as a controlled stress that shifts immune cells toward repair mode. That shift creates the conditions that switch on satellite cells, the stem cells that rebuild muscle.

Wang F, Qin W. Immune metabolic remodeling during exercise rehabilitation: Linking skeletal muscle regeneration, bone homeostasis, and systemic immune adaptation. Tissue Cell. 2026;103:103687. doi:10.1016/j.tice.2026.103687 PubMed

Muscle stem cells weaken with age

Finding. The review found that satellite cells — the muscle's resident stem cells — decline in number and repair power with age, which diminished the muscle's ability to recover from injury. The authors noted the causes in humans are not yet fully understood.

Johnson AL, Bevington RT, Rumelski S, Kassamali N, Parise G. Muscle satellite cells and aging. Am J Physiol Cell Physiol. 2026;331(2):C497-C511. doi:10.1152/ajpcell.00232.2026 PubMed

Why muscle repair slows in aging and chronic disease

Finding. Across aging and several chronic diseases, the review found the same barriers to muscle repair: long-running inflammation, metabolic problems, and scar-like tissue buildup. The authors noted that treatments aimed at several barriers at once looked promising in early studies, but clinical evidence is still limited.

Li X, Zhao Z, Yang J, et al. Mechanisms of Impaired Skeletal Muscle Regeneration and Therapeutic Approaches in Aging and Chronic Disease. Pharmaceuticals (Basel). 2026;19(7). doi:10.3390/ph19071091 PubMed

Tendon Healing and Biologics

TENDON HEALING & BIOLOGICS · 119 FOUND · PULLED 2026-11-02

Tendons and ligaments heal slowly. Biologics such as platelet-rich plasma (PRP) — a concentrate of the patient's own blood platelets — are offered as a way to speed that healing; this topic keeps score of what the trials actually show.

PubMed query: (tendon OR ligament) AND (healing OR regeneration) AND ("platelet-rich plasma" OR "growth factors" OR "stem cells") AND (systematic review[pt] OR meta-analysis[pt])

Added tissue and biologics for rotator cuff repair: what the evidence supports

Finding. Reviewing 419 studies, the authors found the strongest support for human donor grafts and a patient's own tissue in high-risk rotator cuff repairs. Evidence for platelet-rich plasma and stem cells was inconsistent, and no single approach was best for every repair.

Sabry AO, Elmenawi KA, Ibrahim S, et al. Recent concepts in biological augmentation for rotator cuff tears: a scoping review. BMC Musculoskelet Disord. 2026;27(1). doi:10.1186/s12891-026-10436-5 PubMed

Platelet concentrates in ACL reconstruction: the level I evidence check

Finding. Pooling 14 randomized trials (869 patients), the authors found platelet concentrates did not improve patient-reported outcomes or revision rates after ACL reconstruction. They concluded the current level I evidence does not support the add-on.

Migliorini F, Lucenti L, Schäfer L, et al. Autologous platelet concentrate augmentation in anterior cruciate ligament reconstruction: a level I evidence-based meta-analysis. Orthopadie (Heidelb). 2026;55(9):745-754. doi:10.1007/s00132-025-04748-y PubMed

Platelet-rich plasma in ACL reconstruction: a small stability gain, mixed results

Finding. Across 18 randomized trials (1,082 patients), platelet-rich plasma improved measured knee stability but did not significantly improve pain or function scores. The authors called for more high-quality trials.

Serag I, Abouzid M, Hikal H, et al. Platelet-rich plasma in anterior cruciate ligament reconstruction: An updated systematic review and quantitative meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials. Chin J Traumatol. 2026;29(5):358-367. doi:10.1016/j.cjtee.2025.02.008 PubMed
